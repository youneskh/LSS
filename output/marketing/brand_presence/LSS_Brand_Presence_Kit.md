# LSS Brand Presence Kit

## Primary identity

**Brand name:** LSS - Life Sciences Suite  
**Primary slogan:** **Connect Operations. Control Quality. Trace Everything.**  
**Campaign line:** **See the complete journey of a batch.**  
**Positioning line:** Connected enterprise operations and quality management for life sciences.

The logo mark is a connected record path: vertical operations, a quality-status intersection in amber, a trace line, and an end node. It is designed to feel like a controlled process rather than a generic laboratory symbol.

## Logo assets

- `LSS_Logo_Primary.svg`: white backgrounds, proposals, web header, brochure cover.
- `LSS_Logo_Reversed.svg`: dark backgrounds, events, decks, video end cards.
- `LSS_LinkedIn_Banner.svg`: LinkedIn company page banner, 1584 x 396 px.

Keep clear space equal to the height of the amber node. Never stretch, recolour the amber node, add shadows, place the mark on busy photography, or write “Odoo” into the lockup.

## Visual language

| Role | Colour | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Core | `#102A43` | authority, background, primary typography |
| Trace | `#0E7C86` | workflow, links, interaction, movement |
| Decision | `#D98E04` | exception, decision point, important prompt only |
| Field | `#F7FAFC` | clean backgrounds |
| Support | `#486581` / `#D9E2EC` | labels, secondary content |

Typography: IBM Plex Sans or Inter for digital; Arial only for broad compatibility. Use large, calm headlines. Use diagrams, product truth, process records, clean production facilities, and technical detail. Avoid beakers, DNA helices, neon, generic blue “digital transformation” imagery, and unverified product screens.

## Campaign hooks

Use one hook per asset; do not stack slogans.

1. “Can your team explain a batch without opening five systems?”
2. “Your batch story should not have to be reconstructed.”
3. “ERP records explain transactions. Quality records explain decisions.”
4. “The moment a lot is questioned, context matters.”
5. “Make the handoff visible.”
6. “Trace the decision, not just the product.”
7. “When quality needs context, disconnected systems slow everything down.”
8. “From material receipt to quality decision.”
9. “A controlled workflow is a better starting point than another spreadsheet.”
10. “Bring us one difficult workflow.”

## Image direction and production prompts

**Hero image prompt**  
Use case: ads-marketing. Asset: website/deck hero. Scene: clean pharmaceutical manufacturing floor with a production operator and quality reviewer examining a controlled batch record; wide composition with generous left-side negative space for text. Style: cinematic but restrained editorial photography; clinical daylight; navy/teal palette. Avoid: lab coats posed for camera, logos, readable UI, watermark, text, sci-fi effects.

**Traceability image prompt**  
Use case: ads-marketing. Asset: LinkedIn carousel. Scene: close, real-world production-material containers and barcode label area, with subtle teal trace line overlays joining receipt, production, and review contexts. Style: precise enterprise visual; no fictional medical claims; no visible customer branding. Avoid: DNA, holograms, fake product screens.

**Quality workflow image prompt**  
Use case: ads-marketing. Asset: paid social. Scene: quality professional reviewing a controlled document and a manufacturing context board in a modern regulated production office; subject on right, dark negative space on left for the hook. Style: mature European B2B enterprise photography; restrained, credible, precise.

## Presence checklist

**LinkedIn:** banner + primary logo; weekly workflow post; monthly technical carousel; quarterly invitation to a discovery session.  
**Website:** headline, campaign line, one workflow diagram, one industry proof area, clear demo CTA.  
**Events:** dark reverse logo; one 5-stage trace line; headline “See the complete journey of a batch.”  
**Sales:** one primary slogan; end every deck with “Bring one difficult workflow.”

## Claim safeguard

Use: “Designed to support regulated workflows.”  
Never use: “Guaranteed compliance,” “FDA-approved,” “100% compliant,” or any certification claim without documented, approved evidence.
